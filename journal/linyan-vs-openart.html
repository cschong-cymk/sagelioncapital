Same prompt, two machines: what Linyan and OpenArt heard

A small test from the studio while we explore which tools are worth bringing into workshops.

We wanted to see how two different video generators would interpret the same idea: a cat wearing a bow-tie with a small cross, and three pom-poms placed in front of it as a quiet symbol of three-in-one.

Linyan gave us a photorealistic cream cat in a navy bow-tie, the cross hanging clear at the centre, three pom-poms lined up on a sunlit table. The cat bats at them gently. The idea survives intact — direct, almost reverent.

OpenArt heard something else. It produced a soft anime-style piece: a rustic room filled with morning light, close-ups of a sleeping cat, the cat walking toward the lens, then resting in a pair of human hands. Beautiful and emotional, but the Trinity became a mood rather than a symbol.

Both are useful. Linyan is what you reach for when the idea needs to survive the generation. OpenArt is what you reach for when feeling matters more than literal meaning. The lesson for our teaching: the tool shapes the message. Same words, very different results.
